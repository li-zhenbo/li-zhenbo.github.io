\section{Background and Motivation}

The generalized Pad\'e--Lindstedt--Poincar\'e (GPLP) method, first proposed by the authors in Nonlinear Dynamics (2016), successfully eliminated the integration bottleneck of traditional perturbation methods. However, the original GPLP was demonstrated on oscillators with \textbf{symmetric} potentials. Many real oscillators---especially those combining $\Phi^6$ or higher-order polynomial restoring forces with van der Pol damping---exhibit \textbf{asymmetric potentials}, where homoclinic and heteroclinic orbits have fundamentally different geometric structures.

This work extends the GPLP method to handle \textbf{asymmetric potential oscillators} by constructing \textbf{several new types of generalized Pad\'e approximants} specifically tailored to the asymmetry. The result is a significant improvement in both accuracy and applicability.

\section{Methodological Advances}

\subsection{New Generalized Pad\'e Approximants}

The original GPLP used $\cos^{2i}\varphi$ basis for symmetric homoclinic and $\sin^{2i}\varphi$ for heteroclinic orbits. This paper introduces:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Extended asymmetric approximant:} $x(\varphi) = \frac{\sum_{i=0}^{m} \alpha_i (\cos\varphi - c)^{2i}}{\sum_{j=0}^{n} \beta_j (\cos\varphi - c)^{2j}}$ --- where $c$ is a shift parameter capturing potential asymmetry
\item \textbf{Mixed cosine-sine approximant:} Combines both basis sets for cases where the orbit geometry is neither purely symmetric nor antisymmetric
\item \textbf{Adaptive-order approximant:} Dynamically selects $(m,n)$ based on the asymmetry strength and required accuracy
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why Asymmetry Matters}

In oscillators like the $\Phi^6$-Van der Pol system, the potential well is tilted---one saddle is higher than the other. This means:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item The homoclinic orbit looping around the higher saddle has a different shape than that around the lower one
\item Heteroclinic orbits connecting two unequal saddles require an asymmetric basis to capture both endpoints accurately
\item Standard symmetric approximants systematically underestimate the curvature near the higher saddle
\end{itemize}

\section{Applications}

\subsection{Full $\Phi^6$-Van der Pol Oscillator (Asymmetric)}

The most challenging system studied, governed by:
\begin{equation}
\ddot{x} + c_1 x + c_2 x^3 + c_3 x^5 = \varepsilon(1 - x^2)\dot{x}
\label{eq:phi6}
\end{equation}
where $c_1$, $c_2$, $c_3$ are chosen to create an asymmetric double-well potential.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering

The method successfully predicts critical bifurcation values for homoclinic orbits with error below 1\% compared to Runge--Kutta, even for the asymmetric case.

\subsection{Duffing-Harmonic-Van der Pol Oscillator}

For the generalized DH-VDP oscillator with rational restoring force and asymmetric damping, the new approximants handle \textbf{large perturbation parameters} ($\varepsilon = 1$, 2) accurately (Figs.\ 2--4).

Tables 6--8 in the original paper systematically study how homoclinic and heteroclinic bifurcation parameters vary with system parameters---enabled by the analytical nature of GPLP (numerical continuation would be prohibitively expensive).

\section{Key Findings}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Asymmetric GPLP:} New generalized Pad\'e approximants with shift parameters enable accurate homoclinic/heteroclinic solutions for oscillators with asymmetric potentials.
\item \textbf{High accuracy at large $\varepsilon$:} Solutions for $\varepsilon=1$, 2 show error below 2\%---significantly better than the symmetric basis.
\item \textbf{Multi-parameter capability:} The analytical expressions enable systematic parametric studies of bifurcation thresholds, impractical with purely numerical approaches.
\item \textbf{Complement to 2016 method:} This work completes the GPLP framework by covering the asymmetric-potential case, establishing GPLP as a general-purpose tool for strongly nonlinear oscillator bifurcation analysis.
\end{enumerate}
