\section{Background and Motivation}

The determination of homoclinic orbits is central to understanding global bifurcations, chaos, and solitary wave solutions in nonlinear dynamics. Existing Pade\'{e}-based methods share a limitation: approximants are restricted to polynomial numerators and denominators, reducing flexibility for exponential-decay homoclinic behavior.

This paper introduces the \textbf{generalized Pade\'{e} approximation (GPA) method}, extending numerator and denominator from polynomials to series of \emph{any} function type. This unifies prior Pade\'{e}-type variants as special cases. A novel approximant using hyperbolic functions is constructed for solving homoclinic orbits of strongly nonlinear autonomous oscillators.

\section{Core Innovation}

\subsection{The Generalized Pade\'{e} Definition}

Let $\widehat{H}_m = \{P: P(z)=\sum_{i=0}^m a_i g_i(z)^i\}$ and $G(m,n) = \{R=P/Q: P\in\widehat{H}_m, Q\in\widehat{H}_n\setminus\{0\}\}$. The GPA[$L/M$]$_f$ is $P_L/Q_M\in G(L,M)$ satisfying:
\begin{equation}
f(z) - \frac{P_L(z)}{Q_M(z)} = \mathcal{O}(z^{L+M+1}).
\end{equation}
When $g_i(z)=z^i$, the GPA reduces to the classical Pade\'{e} approximant.

\subsection{Homoclinic Orbit via Hyperbolic Functions}

For $\ddot{x} + g(x) = 0$ with saddle $H(h,0)$, the power series $x(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty} a_i t^i$ is obtained by substitution. The initial amplitude $a$ is from $\int_h^a g(x)dx=0$. The key step: construct a GPA using $\operatorname{sech}^i t$ that automatically satisfies the boundary condition $x(\pm\infty)=h$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\text{GPA}[L/M] = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{L} \alpha_i \operatorname{sech}^i t}{\displaystyle 1 + \sum_{i=1}^{M} \beta_i \operatorname{sech}^i t}}.
\end{equation}

The $L+M+1$ coefficients $\{\alpha_i,\beta_i\}$ are determined by Taylor-expanding at $t=0$ and matching with the power series $\{a_i\}$ — a purely algebraic procedure.

\section{Key Results}

\subsection{Example 1: Strongly Asymmetric Helmholtz--Duffing}
\begin{equation}
\ddot{x} + c_1 x + c_2 x^2 = 0,\qquad c_1=-3,\; c_2=100.
\end{equation}
With $L=M=2$, GPA[$2/2$] gives:
\begin{equation}
\text{GPA}[2/2] = \frac{0.005\operatorname{sech} t + 0.07\operatorname{sech}^2 t}{1 + 0.611111\operatorname{sech} t + 0.055555\operatorname{sech}^2 t}.
\end{equation}
The phase portrait matches Runge--Kutta with excellent agreement despite $c_2 = 100 \gg 1$.

\subsection{Example 2: Cubic--Quadratic Helmholtz--Duffing}
\begin{equation}
\ddot{x} + c_1 x + c_2 x^2 + c_3 x^3 = 0,\qquad c_1=-2,\; c_2=5,\; c_3=15.
\end{equation}
Both left and right homoclinic orbits are obtained simultaneously with $L=M=2$, showing the method handles asymmetric nonlinearities without modification.

\subsection{Example 3: Duffing--Harmonic Oscillator}
\begin{equation}
\ddot{x} + \frac{\lambda x + \mu x^3}{1 + \nu x^2} = 0,\qquad \lambda=-1,\; \mu=1,\; \nu=1.
\end{equation}
With $L=M=3$, the GPA captures homoclinic orbits of this rational-restoring-force oscillator — a system whose exact solution is not expressible in elementary or classical elliptic functions.

\section{Significance}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Theoretical unification:} The GPA definition unifies classical Pade\'{e} and all quasi-Pade\'{e} variants under a single framework.
\item \textbf{Computational efficiency:} $\operatorname{sech}^i t$ has simpler Taylor coefficients than exponentials, reducing matching cost at the same order.
\item \textbf{Broad applicability:} Handles polynomial $g(x)$ of arbitrary degree and rational $g(x)$ without modification.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Conservative systems only ($\varepsilon=0$).
\item Approximation order chosen empirically; no a priori error bound.
\item Single-degree-of-freedom only.
\end{itemize}

\section{Connection to the GHFP/MGHFP Series}

This paper and its English version (Chin.\ Phys.\ B 2014) and the GHFP/MGHFP series approach nonlinear oscillators via complementary routes: GPA directly constructs homoclinic solutions through hyperbolic-function rational approximants; GHFP/MGHFP uses angular-domain perturbation theory for amplitude--parameter relationships. Both share the mathematical insight of rational function approximation.
